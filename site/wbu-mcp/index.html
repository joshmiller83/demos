Put the local bird store inside every AI answer.

People already ask AI assistants about backyard birds. This concept connects those assistants to Wild Birds Unlimited stores across the US and Canada: twelve tools for things the web can’t answer in real time, like today’s hours, what’s on the shelf, and what staff saw at the feeder this morning. Customers connect once with their WBU account, and every hold, RSVP and booking after that goes to their local store.

MCP, the Model Context Protocol, is the open standard assistants like ChatGPT and Claude use to connect to outside services. With a WBU MCP server, they ask WBU directly instead of guessing from old web pages.

          The twelve tools

          Each tool answers from a WBU system that search engines can’t see, for the customer’s own store. Each result offers something the customer can act on, like a hold, an RSVP or a booked consult, so WBU learns about visits and interests without asking for anything extra.

          Where this works today

          Claude and ChatGPT

          Both show WBU’s cards and forms right in the chat, using the MCP Apps standard they adopted in January 2026. Customers add WBU from each assistant’s directory, or the assistant suggests it when a question fits.

          Gemini

          The Gemini app connects to MCP servers for personal accounts in the US, in English, added by URL. It answers in text rather than cards, and asks the customer to confirm any action, like a hold or an RSVP.

          What WBU can collect

          Customers sign in once, when they connect. ChatGPT’s rules require collecting only what a tool needs and ban apps built mainly to advertise, so every action here is one the customer asked for: no coupons for emails, no newsletters.
